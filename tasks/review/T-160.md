## Why

Provider-services deals are priced on collections that depend on payer contracts, denial behaviour and clinicians staying credentialed — none of which the generic platform checks. This is the parent pack for the clinics and hospitals variants.

## What this pack adds (the delta only)

1. **Payer-mix and collections verification** (`packages/calculations`): billed vs collected by payer recomputed from remittance/billing exports and reconciled to the P&L by year; denial rate and days-in-AR as trend findings.
2. **Credentialing register:** every clinician on the roster against licence and credential status/expiry from documents; practice on an expired licence is a high-severity finding.
3. **Clinician concentration:** share of collections by clinician from exports; concentration without a non-compete or retention document is a finding.
4. **Compliance evidence presence:** HIPAA risk assessment, coding/billing audit reports and payer contract register (with expiry windows) as evidence; absence shown as absence.

## Guardrails

- **No clinical or legal judgment:** the pack checks documents, dates and arithmetic; it never assesses quality of care or legal compliance.
- **Evidence and absence:** figures come only from documents and adapters; absence is shown as absence, never filled in.

## Multi-year P&L and trend (Kumar, 2026-11-26 — every pack)
The pack must take imported P&L statements for multiple fiscal years through the platform import system (statements by year) and use all of them: QoE and the valuation bridge read every imported year, and the pack produces trend findings — revenue growth, gross/operating margin drift, and the vertical's key expense ratios across years — each figure traceable to the import it came from. A single-year import still works (trend findings simply report one year). Withdrawn imports are excluded from trend reads like every other read path. The demo data includes at least three years of P&L with one planted trend defect that a single-year view would miss, and the acceptance test proves it surfaces.
