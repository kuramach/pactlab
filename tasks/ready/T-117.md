# T-117 — Buyer financing: how the buyer funds the deal, disclosed to the seller

Model: opus
Engine: claude
**Lane:** core
**Context:** deep
**Touches:** packages/domain (pact model, terms), packages/db, packages/contracts, apps/api (financing panel, evidence types), apps/web (buyer financing panel, seller view, seller-led bidder comparison), docs (ADR)
**Needs:** nothing — builds on the documents/evidence machinery, the proposed-terms model, and the ADR 0022 close (funds-transfer record)

## Why

Gap confirmed 2026-11-10 (Kumar's question; verified against the spec, deal
contract, pact sections, phases, intake checklist, and ADR 0022): the flow has
no buyer-financing piece at all. The only funds element is at close, where the
buyer records the payment and the seller confirms receipt. A seller deciding
whether to commit to a buyer — or comparing bidders in a seller-led process —
cannot see how the buyer will fund the transaction: cash vs SBA 7(a) vs
conventional debt vs seller note, proof of funds, or lender status. That is
the seller's core deal-certainty question, and today it happens entirely
outside the product.

## What

1. **Financing panel (buyer-maintained), one per pact.** A set of tranches,
   each: kind (CASH, SBA_7A, CONVENTIONAL_DEBT, SELLER_NOTE, ROLLOVER_EQUITY,
   OTHER), amount, and status on a ladder: NOT_STARTED -> EXPLORING ->
   PRE_QUALIFIED -> APPLICATION_SUBMITTED -> COMMITMENT_ISSUED ->
   CLEARED_TO_CLOSE. Plus key dates the seller cares about (e.g. commitment
   expected by). The panel shows a coverage check against the currently
   agreed price: total tranches vs price, flagging under/over-coverage.
2. **Documents as evidence.** Proof of funds, lender pre-qualification and
   commitment letters, and SBA pre-qualification attach as documents on the
   relevant tranche. Uploads reuse the existing documents machinery; a
   tranche's status above PRE_QUALIFIED should be backable by an attached
   document, and the UI says so when it is not.
3. **Seller view.** The seller sees the structure (kinds, amounts, statuses,
   dates, coverage) and the documents the buyer has shared — and nothing
   else. The buyer's underlying personal/business financial documents are
   buyer-confidential unless the buyer explicitly shares that document;
   sharing is per-document and revocable, consistent with how the product
   treats counterparty-confidential material elsewhere (buyer personas,
   the confirmed-value ledger).
4. **Deal-certainty signal.** The seller's pact view carries a plain
   financing posture derived from the panel (e.g. "Commitment issued —
   $X of $Y covered", "No lender engaged"), and financing gaps surface as
   pact blockers (e.g. commitment date passed with no commitment) in the
   same places other blockers appear, including the T-110 reports read
   model (seller reports show the seller-visible layer only).
5. **Financing contingency term.** Add FINANCING_CONTINGENCY to the
   proposed-term types alongside PRICE_REDUCTION / REMEDIATION_COST /
   ESCROW / INDEMNITY, so a financing contingency with a deadline can be
   proposed, agreed, and tracked into the ADR 0022 close, whose
   funds-transfer record remains the end of the chain.
6. **Seller-led processes (ADR 0015).** A seller comparing bidders sees a
   per-bidder financing summary (structure + status + coverage only).
   Bidder isolation holds: no bidder sees another bidder's financing, and
   financing data never leaks across pacts.

7. **Commitment cash in escrow (earnest money).** The panel also records
   whether the buyer is putting commitment cash into escrow: amount, escrow
   holder (title company, attorney, broker — a third party, never Pactlab),
   deposit-due date, the date the money goes hard (non-refundable), refund
   conditions in plain text, and a status ladder: NOT_COMMITTED -> COMMITTED
   -> DEPOSITED -> APPLIED_AT_CLOSE / RELEASED_TO_SELLER / REFUNDED_TO_BUYER.
   The deposit itself is recorded the way ADR 0022 records the closing
   payment — buyer records date, reference, and optional shared proof;
   seller confirms or disputes — and the escrow state feeds the item-4
   certainty signal ("Earnest $X deposited, hard after <date>"). This is
   distinct from the existing ESCROW term type, which is a remediation
   holdback arising from findings; the two can coexist on the same pact and
   the close flow reconciles both (earnest applies against the price at
   close; its disposition on a terminated deal follows the recorded refund
   conditions).

## Guardrails

- Financing is about certainty of close, not price: it does NOT enter the
  valuation bridge and never changes findings, directions, or the agreed
  price on its own. If implementation proposes any financing-derived
  finding type, it must register in VALUATION_DIRECTION_MAP with an
  explicit direction and rationale, like every other evidence type.
- No lender/bank integrations, no credit checks, no loan origination:
  statuses are buyer-attested, documents are buyer-uploaded. Integrate,
  don't build, if a future need appears.
- Amounts and statuses are attestations, labelled as buyer-provided
  wherever the seller sees them; document-backed statuses are visually
  distinguishable from unattested ones.

## Acceptance

- Buyer can create/edit tranches (kinds above), move statuses, attach
  documents, and share/unshare documents per-document.
- Seller view shows structure/status/coverage and shared documents only;
  tests prove unshared financing documents and buyer financials are
  invisible to the seller, and that bidder A's financing is invisible to
  bidder B in a seller-led pact.
- Coverage check flags tranches totalling below the agreed price.
- A financing contingency can be proposed and agreed as a term and shows
  in the close checklist (ADR 0022 flow) ahead of the funds-transfer
  record.
- Financing posture appears on the seller's pact view and in the detailed
  pact report (T-110) for the seller's org; the buyer-confidential layer
  appears in no seller-visible surface, proven by report text-extraction
  tests like the existing confidentiality tests.
- An escrow commitment can be recorded (amount, holder, due date, hard
  date, refund conditions), its deposit recorded with proof and confirmed
  or disputed by the seller, and its disposition (applied / released /
  refunded) shows in the close checklist; the seller-visible posture
  includes the escrow state.
- An ADR records the visibility model (what the seller sees by default,
  what is per-document shared, what never leaves the buyer's org).
