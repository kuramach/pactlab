# Reseller certification — tutorial scripts and exam question bank

Content source for T-191. Kumar records the videos in his own voice over the live product; scripts below are written to be read aloud, with [ON SCREEN] cues for the walkthrough path. Target ~5 minutes per module. The question bank is the exam's source of truth — answers and rationales are admin-only and never shown to candidates before an attempt.

Standing facts used throughout (do not drift from these):
- Tagline: "AI rigor. Human judgment."
- Pricing: per-deal $5,000 fixed + variable on enterprise value — 5 bps on the first $25M, 2 bps on $25M–$100M, 1 bp above $100M, variable capped at $50,000. Pilot is a flat $5,000 with no variable.
- Reseller commissions: 15% / 10% / 5% of collected revenue in years 1 / 2 / 3, on amounts actually collected after discounts. Clients get 10% off year one when they come through a reseller. Attribution is first-tag-wins. Commissions are clawed back if the payment is refunded within 30 days. Payouts run through Stripe Connect. Commission numbers are never public.
- Hard rule: resellers never receive diligence content — no findings, no deal documents, no data-room material, ever.

## Module 1 — What Pactlab is, and who it is for (~5 min)

[ON SCREEN: pactlab.ai homepage]

"Welcome to Pactlab — and thank you for wanting to represent us. I'm Kumar, founder. Before you talk to a single client, I want you to understand what we actually do, because the fastest way to lose a deal — and your certification — is to describe Pactlab as something it isn't.

[ON SCREEN: the core loop diagram]

"Pactlab is a deal platform for private equity firms and corporate development teams. It manages an acquisition end to end: diligence, valuation, deal terms and closing, in one place. The flow is always the same. Evidence comes in — financials, contracts, operational data. Pactlab normalizes it, turns it into reviewable findings, connects those findings to valuation, and from valuation to the actual deal terms a buyer proposes. Every step is traceable back to the evidence it came from. That's our tagline in practice: AI rigor, human judgment. The AI does the grinding; the deal team makes the calls.

[ON SCREEN: industry packs page]

"Pactlab ships with diligence packs for different kinds of companies — software, multifamily real estate, healthcare, energy, and more. Each pack knows what to look for in that industry: the metrics that matter, the documents to expect, the defects that typically hide. When you qualify a prospect, the first question is always: what kind of company are they acquiring? If there's a pack for it, the conversation gets very concrete, very fast.

[ON SCREEN: who it's for]

"Who buys Pactlab? The buyer is a PE firm or a corporate acquirer doing real deals — not a broker, not a seller shopping for a valuation opinion, not a student. Your job is introductions to people who run acquisitions. One more thing, and it's absolute: you will never see what's inside a client's deal. No findings, no documents, no data room. That wall is why clients trust the platform, and it's why they can trust you. Module 4 covers the rules in full. Next: how Pactlab is priced — because you'll be asked, and you need the math cold."

## Module 2 — Pricing, worked live (~5 min)

[ON SCREEN: pricing page]

"Every reseller gets asked two questions: what does it cost, and what do I earn? This module makes you dangerous on both.

[ON SCREEN: the price formula]

"Pactlab is priced per deal. Five thousand dollars fixed, plus a variable based on the size of the deal — the enterprise value. The variable is five basis points — that's five hundredths of one percent — on the first twenty-five million, two basis points from twenty-five to a hundred million, and one basis point above a hundred million. The variable is capped at fifty thousand dollars. Let's do the math together, because you'll do it in front of clients.

"A ten-million-dollar deal: the fixed five thousand, plus five basis points on ten million — another five thousand. Total: ten thousand dollars.
"A fifty-million-dollar deal: five thousand fixed, twelve and a half thousand on the first twenty-five million, five thousand on the next twenty-five. Total: twenty-two thousand, five hundred.
"A hundred-million-dollar deal totals thirty-two thousand, five hundred. And because of the cap, no deal ever prices above fifty-five thousand all-in.
"One exception: a pilot deal is a flat five thousand, no variable. Pilots are how most clients start — say so early; it lowers the temperature of the whole conversation.

[ON SCREEN: checkout with promo and partner fields]

"Two different codes can appear at checkout, and clients confuse them, so don't. A promo code is a price promotion — it changes what the client pays. Your partner code is attribution — it records that the client came through you. They're separate fields, both can be used, and the effects are itemized separately on the receipt. Your commission is calculated on what is actually collected, after any discounts — Module 3 has the details.

[ON SCREEN: the comparison anchor]

"Why does this pricing land? Because the alternative — traditional diligence support — runs fifty to three hundred thousand dollars a deal. You're not selling software; you're selling a diligence process at a tenth of the going rate, with the receipts to prove every number. Never invent figures beyond what's in your kit. If a client asks something you can't answer from the materials, the correct answer is: 'I'll get you the exact answer from Pactlab' — then ask us."

## Module 3 — How referrals and commissions work (~4 min)

[ON SCREEN: reseller portal dashboard]

"This module is the machinery: how you get credited, and how you get paid.

[ON SCREEN: referral link and partner code]

"When you're certified, your portal activates two things: your personal referral link and your partner code. Attribution is first-tag-wins. The first reseller whose link or code touches a client owns that client relationship for commission purposes — permanently. If a client clicked your link in March and a colleague's in June, the client is yours. This is why speed matters, and why you should get your link into a prospect's hands the moment a conversation turns real — not after the demo, not next week.

[ON SCREEN: commission schedule]

"Your commission is fifteen percent of collected revenue in year one, ten percent in year two, five percent in year three. 'Collected' is the operative word: commission accrues when the client's payment lands, on the amount actually collected after discounts — not on quotes, not on invoices, not on promises. Clients who come through you also get ten percent off their first year; that's your door-opener, use it.

[ON SCREEN: Stripe Connect]

"Payouts run through Stripe Connect, which you set up during onboarding. Two rules to know cold. First, clawbacks: if a client's payment is refunded within thirty days, the commission on it is reversed — after thirty days, it's yours to keep. Second, discretion: commission percentages are your private contract terms. They never appear in public materials, and you never quote them to clients. A client who asks what you earn gets a polite deflection, not a number.

[ON SCREEN: the dashboard]

"Your dashboard shows your attributed clients, collected revenue, and pending and paid commissions — in the currency the client paid, with any payout conversion shown with its rate and date. If the dashboard and your expectations ever disagree, ask us before you guess. Next module is the one that keeps you certified: the rules of the road."

## Module 4 — The rules of the road (~5 min)

[ON SCREEN: rules summary card]

"This module is short because the rules are few. Break one, and you can lose certification — in one case, immediately.

"Rule one: the confidentiality wall. You never receive diligence content. Not findings, not financials, not contracts, not a peek at the data room — not from the client, not from us. If a client offers to send you deal documents — and they will, because they trust you — your answer is scripted: 'Please don't send those to me. Upload them into your pact in Pactlab, where they're protected.' Then tell us it happened. This isn't bureaucracy; it's the reason clients can trust the platform with a live deal.

"Rule two: claims discipline. You may say what your kit says — nothing more. No invented statistics, no customer names or results you can't point to in the materials, no promised features and no dates. If it isn't in the kit, it isn't said. When a prospect pushes, 'I'll confirm that with Pactlab' is always an acceptable answer.

"Rule three: stay in your lane. You make introductions and help a client get started. You do not advise on the deal itself, interpret findings, or negotiate terms — that's the client's team and Pactlab's product. A reseller who starts opining on valuation stops being a channel and starts being a liability.

"Rule four: one client, one reseller. First-tag-wins means no poaching: if a client is already attributed, you don't pitch around the incumbent reseller — you tell us, and we sort it out.

"Rule five: keep your certification current. Pricing and rules change; when they do materially, you'll be asked to re-certify on the new version before you represent us again. Your certificate is stamped with the version you passed.

"That's the whole code. Pass the exam, and your link goes live. Welcome aboard — now go earn it."

## Exam administration (for the build, T-191)

- Draw: 25 questions per attempt, stratified — at least 5 from Section B and 5 from Section D; remainder across A and C. Order and option order shuffled.
- Pass mark: 80% (20/25). Section B is compute-only: no partial credit.
- Failure: 24-hour cooldown, then a retake with a fresh draw. All attempts and scores recorded; Kumar sees them in the admin console.
- Open-book: candidates may re-watch modules between attempts, not during a timed attempt (30-minute limit).
- The certificate records the exam version; material changes to pricing or rules publish a new version and trigger re-certification (see D7).
