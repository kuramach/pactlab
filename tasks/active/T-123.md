# T-123 — Org brand kit: extract logo, colors, and look from the company website; brand the app, documents, and emails

Model: opus
Engine: claude
**Lane:** platform
**Context:** deep
**Touches:** packages/domain + packages/db (org brand kit), apps/api (org setup, extraction job, report/document generation, email templates), apps/web (theming, logo, sign-in), docs (spec note)
**Needs:** T-110 (reports — branding hooks into its generation), T-096 (queued jobs — extraction runs as one; a compatible inline seam is acceptable)

## Why

Kumar's ask (2026-11-13): when a pact is created for an org, ask for
the company's website; if provided, extract the logo, look and feel,
and colors, and use that branding where it belongs — his examples:
the logo at top left when they log in, and printed reports carrying
the brand of whichever side (buyer or seller) is logged in. A deal
platform that firms run their acquisitions through should feel like
*their* system, not a vendor's. Branding also builds trust with
counterparties receiving documents and invitations.

## What

1. **Capture at setup.** Org creation (and the first-pact flow) asks
   for the company website URL — optional, skippable, editable later
   in org settings. Providing it kicks off extraction; skipping it
   leaves the org on the default Pactlab brand kit.
2. **Extraction (queued job).** Fetch the site and build a draft
   brand kit: logo (prefer schema.org / og:image / header logo;
   capture light and dark variants where the site offers them),
   primary and accent colors (theme-color meta, CSS, logo palette),
   and the site's primary font family mapped to the nearest font the
   app supports. Store the kit on the org with an extraction
   confidence note. Extraction is best-effort: failure or a poor
   result never blocks setup and never overwrites a human edit.
3. **Brand kit is org-admin editable.** Org settings show the kit
   with previews (app header, report cover, email header) and allow:
   upload/replace logo, adjust colors, pick the font, revert to the
   Pactlab default. Manual edits always win over re-extraction;
   re-running extraction requires an explicit admin action and shows
   a diff before applying.
4. **Where the branding applies** (the full surface list):
   - **App chrome:** org logo at top left in place of the Pactlab
     logo; accent color drives primary buttons, links, and chart
     accents; favicon and browser tab title use the org logo/name.
   - **Sign-in:** the org's logo and colors on its sign-in screens.
   - **Printed / PDF documents:** executive and detailed pact
     reports (T-110), the IC pack (T-118), and the LOI document
     (T-119) — cover page, headers/footers, logo, colors, font.
   - **Emails:** invitations, notifications, and sign-in emails
     carry the sending org's logo and accent in the header.
   - **Counterparty-shared artifacts:** a document or report shared
     across orgs shows the *generating* org's brand.
   Branding rules: in-app surfaces always follow the **viewing**
   user's active org; generated documents carry the **generating**
   org's brand, stamped at generation time (consistent with T-110's
   immutable versions — a later rebrand never rewrites an old
   report). Multi-org users see the brand switch with org context.
   Generated documents and emails keep a discreet "Powered by
   Pactlab" footer line. The Pactlab marketing site is NOT branded —
   it stays Pactlab's own.
5. **Accessibility and sanity rails.** Accent colors are checked for
   contrast against the surfaces they land on; a failing color is
   adjusted to the nearest passing shade and the admin is told.
   Logos are normalized (trimmed, sized, transparent-background
   preferred) and stored as proper brand assets, not hotlinked from
   the customer's site.

## Guardrails

- Fetching an arbitrary customer URL is an SSRF surface: reuse the
  repo's existing SSRF-guard patterns (as in the Jira connector) —
  scheme/host validation, no internal/loopback targets, size and
  time caps, fetch only public pages and assets.
- A brand kit is presentation data only: it never appears in
  findings, valuation, or terms, and one org's kit is never exposed
  to another org except as rendered on documents that org generated
  and shared (which is the point of branding them).
- No website, no problem: every surface must render correctly on the
  default Pactlab kit, which remains the fallback everywhere.

## Acceptance

- Creating an org with a website URL produces a draft brand kit
  (logo + colors + font) without blocking setup; an admin can edit
  every element and preview the app header, a report cover, and an
  email header before saving.
- Logged in as that org: top-left logo, accent theming, favicon, and
  sign-in screens all show the org's brand; switching orgs switches
  the brand.
- A T-110 report and a T-119 LOI generated under the org carry its
  logo/colors/font and the "Powered by Pactlab" footer; a version
  generated before a rebrand is unchanged.
- An invitation email from the org carries its branding.
- A site that fails extraction (unreachable, no logo found, blocked)
  leaves the org fully functional on the default kit, with the
  failure visible to the admin and a manual-upload path offered.
