# T-122 — Seller pre-sale structuring readiness: grantor trust (IDGT) and neighbouring strategies

Model: opus
Engine: claude
**Lane:** core
**Context:** deep
**Touches:** packages/domain (pact sections, parties), packages/db (structuring records, evidence), apps/api (pact lifecycle, closing), apps/web (seller structuring section), docs (spec note)
**Needs:** T-119 (LOI — seller-of-record and parties flow from it), T-110-style confidentiality tests

## Why

Direction from Kumar (2026-11-13): research grantor trusts in
small-business sales and add support to the pact to help the seller.
Research findings (sourced report on file, 2026-11-13):

- The pre-sale play is an **intentionally defective grantor trust
  (IDGT)**: the owner gifts seed money (~10%, practitioner convention)
  to an irrevocable grantor trust, then sells business interests to
  the trust for an installment note at >= AFR. Under Rev. Rul. 85-13
  that sale is disregarded for income tax (grantor dealing with
  themselves); when the trust later sells to the real buyer, the
  **grantor personally pays the income tax on the gain** (the "tax
  burn", Rev. Rul. 2004-64: not a gift, uses no exemption). What the
  strategy actually saves is **estate tax**: appreciation above the
  note passes to heirs outside the estate. It saves zero income tax
  on the sale itself.
- It only pays when the seller's projected estate clears the federal
  exclusion (**$15M per person / $30M per couple in 2026**, OBBBA made
  it permanent) or a state estate tax (from $1M in OR, $2M in MA).
  Below that it can leave heirs worse off: fees, irrevocability, and
  **no section 1014 step-up** on trust assets (Rev. Rul. 2023-2).
- **Timing is the whole game.** No statutory minimum period and no
  bright-line test, but the transfer must precede a binding agreement
  and ideally any LOI (practitioner lead times run 6-18 months). A
  pending deal price kills the valuation discounts (Reg.
  section 25.2512-1) and late transfers invite assignment-of-income /
  step-transaction attacks (*Ferguson*, 9th Cir. 1999; *Estate of
  Hoensheid*, T.C. Memo. 2023-34 — gift two days before closing).
  Section 2036 is the main unwind risk if the note is not bona fide
  and actually serviced.
- Entity traps to surface: an S-corp IDGT shareholder gets only a
  2-year post-death grace period before the S election terminates
  unless QSST/ESBT planning is in place (section 1361(c)(2)); a
  grantor trust gets **no separate section 1202 QSBS cap** (stacking
  needs non-grantor trusts).

Sellers typically learn about this after the LOI, when it is too
late. Pactlab's help is timing, tracking, and record-keeping — early
surfacing, an advisor-run checklist held as pact evidence, and the
trust correctly reflected as seller of record at signing and close.
**Pactlab gives no tax or legal advice.**

## What

1. **Seller-side structuring section** in the pact (seller org only),
   available from pact creation, with a **structuring window**
   indicator driven by the deal timeline: OPEN before any LOI,
   AT RISK once an LOI is issued, CLOSED once a binding agreement
   exists — labelled as planning prompts for the seller's advisors,
   never as legal deadlines or safe harbours.
2. **Strategy tracks** as checklist templates with steps, owners, and
   evidence slots. Primary track: **IDGT pre-sale sale** — trust
   drafted (independent trustee; swap power; any tax-reimbursement
   clause present at inception, per CCA 202352018), EIN and trust
   accounts, seed gift + Form 709, qualified appraisal (pre-LOI),
   sale-to-trust agreement + note (rate vs AFR, schedule), entity
   consents and assignments (incl. S-corp QSST/ESBT readiness),
   note payments recorded as they are serviced. Secondary tracks as
   lighter checklists: charitable remainder trust, section 453
   installment sale to the buyer, section 1202 QSBS eligibility
   check, state-tax trust note (with the NY/CA statutory caveat).
3. **Advisor seats**: the seller's CPA and estate attorney join as
   seller-side participants who mark steps confirmed; attestations
   are timestamped pact records, the same discipline as other pact
   confirmations.
4. **Seller of record**: when a transfer completes, the pact's seller
   party can become the trust — trustee signs through the existing
   DocuSign flow, T-119 LOI parties reflect the trust, and the
   closing flow records the note repayment from proceeds as a
   closing item (distinct from price and terms). A seller-private
   record holds the CPA's modelled tax-burn reserve figure (recorded
   only; the platform computes nothing).

## Guardrails

- The platform never recommends a strategy, computes a tax figure,
  drafts a trust document, or states that a structure is valid.
  Standing notice wherever the section appears: "Pactlab does not
  provide tax or legal advice. Structuring decisions belong to the
  seller's CPA and estate attorney."
- Hard confidentiality wall, buyer side sees none of it: checklists,
  tracks, attestations, and the burn record never appear in buyer
  views, bidder summaries, or reports (proven by text-extraction
  tests, as in T-110/T-118). The buyer sees only the seller of
  record's identity, which signing and payment require anyway.
- Structuring never enters the valuation bridge, findings, or terms;
  no VALUATION_DIRECTION_MAP changes.
- Lead times are shown as practitioner rules of thumb with sources
  labelled as such; the only hard statement is the settled one:
  transfers must precede a binding agreement to work as intended.

## Acceptance

- On a demo pact, the seller can open the structuring section, pick
  the IDGT track, and work the checklist end to end with evidence
  attached and advisor attestations recorded.
- The window indicator flips OPEN -> AT RISK when an LOI is issued
  on the pact (T-119) and -> CLOSED at a binding agreement, with the
  change logged.
- Completing the transfer step swaps the seller of record to the
  trust; the LOI parties, the signing envelope, and the closing
  payment records all show the trust/trustee, and note repayment
  appears as its own closing item.
- Buyer-side UI and generated reports contain zero structuring
  content (text-extraction tests in the PR).
- The no-advice notice renders on every structuring surface.
