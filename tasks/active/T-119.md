# T-119 — Letter of intent generated from the agreed terms (binding vs non-binding)

Model: opus
Engine: claude
**Lane:** core
**Context:** deep
**Touches:** packages/domain (terms, pact lifecycle), packages/db, packages/contracts, apps/api (LOI generation), apps/web (LOI draft/review/send flow), packages/connectors (existing DocuSign signing adapter), docs (ADR)
**Needs:** T-107 (two-sided agreement, e-signature — the LOI sits immediately before it and reuses its signing path)

## Why

External review (Kumar's PE contact, 2026-11-11): "Intent letter —
binding and non-binding." Verified against the spec: there is no letter
of intent anywhere in the product. The flow jumps from proposed/agreed
terms to the definitive two-sided agreement at close. In practice the
LOI is the bridge artifact: it fixes the headline economics as
*non-binding* intent while making exclusivity, confidentiality, and
process *binding* — and both sides sign it on that basis.

## What

1. **Generation from recorded state.** The LOI is generated from the
   pact's agreed terms and key facts — parties, price/consideration,
   structure, seller-note or financing references (T-117 posture where
   relevant), diligence scope and timeline, closing conditions,
   exclusivity period. Values are pulled from the term records verbatim;
   nothing is re-typed, and the generated LOI is a versioned, immutable
   document (PDF, reports machinery) once sent.
2. **Two clearly separated parts.**
   - *Non-binding provisions* (labelled as such in the document):
     price and structure, timeline, diligence, conditions to close —
     statements of intent only, no obligation to close.
   - *Binding provisions* (labelled as such): exclusivity / no-shop
     with its period, confidentiality, governing law, expenses,
     counterparts/electronic signatures. Signature binds only this part,
     and the document states that plainly at the signature block.
3. **Signing.** Sent for e-signature through the existing DocuSign
   integration (as the pact creator's account, per ADR 0022): buyer
   signs, seller countersigns. Statuses: DRAFT -> SENT -> COUNTERSIGNED
   / DECLINED / EXPIRED. The exclusivity end date is tracked on the
   pact; expiry without a countersignature surfaces as a pact blocker/
   timeline event, and a countersigned LOI satisfies the run-up to the
   T-107 agreement step.
4. **Template discipline.** One maintained LOI template with named
   fields (exclusivity days, governing law, expense language) edited as
   values, not free-form AI drafting of legal prose. The draft screen
   shows binding vs non-binding side by side before sending, and the
   template carries a standing "have your counsel review" notice.

## Guardrails

- The LOI never invents terms: any value not in the pact's records is a
  blank the user fills explicitly, shown as unfilled in the draft.
- A sent LOI version is immutable; changes mean a new version and a new
  envelope. Prior versions stay on the pact record.
- Buyer-confidential material (persona, confirmed-value ledger, IC
  content from T-118) never enters the LOI — it is a counterparty-facing
  document built only from agreed terms and shared facts.
- Pactlab generates and routes the document; it is not legal advice and
  does not act as either party's counsel — the notice ships in the
  product, not just the doc.

## Acceptance

- From a pact with agreed terms, a user generates an LOI whose price,
  structure, and dates match the term records exactly (test compares
  generated content to the records, including PDF text extraction).
- Binding and non-binding provisions are separate, labelled sections in
  the document and in the pre-send review screen.
- The LOI can be sent via the existing signing adapter; countersigned /
  declined / expired states land on the pact timeline, and exclusivity
  expiry is tracked and surfaced.
- A countersigned LOI is visible to both parties on the pact; drafts
  are visible only to the drafting side until sent (tests).
- An ADR records the LOI's place in the flow (after terms, before the
  T-107 definitive agreement) and the binding/non-binding model.

## Built (2026-11-13)
- **Design:** ADR 0030.
- **Kumar's choices:** optional (switchable per pact), in-app or DocuSign signing, generated from the agreed terms.
- **Tests:** API tests cover drafting, visibility, the verbatim price in the PDF, signing order, exclusivity, expiry and the gate.
- **e-signature:** the envelope path reuses T-107's, and the existing e-signature tests still pass.
