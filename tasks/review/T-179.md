## Why

Kumar (2026-11-26): buyers walk in with deal breakers — for a multifamily deal, the cap rate must clear X, occupancy must have held 90%+ for the last 3 years, and so on. Today those live in partners' heads and get checked by hand, late. Deal Criticals make them **org-level policy**: the org writes its critical parameters once, and for every pact the platform scours all the evidence — documents, imports, multi-year financials — and rates each critical **Good, Even or Bad**, with the evidence shown. It is the buyer's investment criteria made machine-checkable, and it feeds the investment-committee step (T-118) instead of duplicating it.

## Build

1. **The org library.** An org keeps its criticals in Settings, scoped per company type (cap rate and occupancy belong to Multifamily; churn and NRR to SaaS — a critical only evaluates on pacts of its type, plus an "all types" scope). A critical is written **in the org's own words** ("Occupancy at or above 90% in each of the last 3 years") and/or structured (metric, comparator, threshold, lookback period, and an optional **Even band** — how close counts as borderline, e.g. within 2 points). When the org writes words, AI proposes the structured form and **the user confirms it before it can ever evaluate** — the confirmed structure, displayed next to their words, is the only thing the engine runs; an unconfirmed critical is inert. The metric vocabulary comes from the packs (each pack registers the metrics it can evidence — cap rate, occupancy, DSCR, payer mix, take rate, and the rest of its claims-register metrics), so a critical can only target a metric the platform knows how to look for.
2. **Evaluation — per pact, evidence or nothing.** For each pact, every applicable critical is evaluated against everything in the pact: deterministic recomputation first where the metric is calculable from imports and structured data (occupancy and cap rate from the rent roll and multi-year P&L via packages/calculations — trend criticals read **all imported years**, per the multi-year rule), AI extraction with page citations where the metric lives in document text. Ratings:
   - **Good** — the threshold is met on the evidence.
   - **Even** — inside the critical's Even band, or met on part of the required period (2 of the last 3 years), or the evidence is mixed across sources.
   - **Bad** — the threshold is missed on the evidence.
   - **No evidence** — a distinct fourth state, never folded into Even: the pact simply does not contain what this critical needs, and the panel says exactly what document would settle it (which also feeds document requests).
   Every rating carries its receipts: the figures used, the recomputation or the cited pages, and the period covered. A rating without evidence behind it cannot be produced.
3. **The Criticals panel.** On the pact: one row per critical — the org's words, the confirmed threshold, the rating, the key figures (with a per-year trend for lookback criticals), and click-through to the evidence. Re-evaluation is explicit (new documents or imports trigger a "stale" marker, not a silent re-run), and each evaluation stamps the critical's version.
4. **Consequences through the existing machinery.** A Bad critical raises a finding (direction −1) that flows into the valuation bridge and term proposals under VALUATION_DIRECTION_MAP, like any drag. A Good critical is +1 — cushion rules apply: it confirms value in the buyer-confidential ledger, never raises price. Even and No evidence are 0 — informational, with their rationale. Criticals are **buyer-confidential**: the seller never sees the org's criticals, thresholds or ratings (same wall as personas and the confirmed-value ledger). Personas do not alter criticals or their ratings — personas translate findings into terms; criticals are the org's own policy.
5. **Versioning.** Editing a critical creates a new version; past pact evaluations keep the version they were stamped with, and the panel shows when a pact was rated under an older version of the org's policy.


## Two levels: org standards and pact criticals (Kumar, 2026-11-26)

Criticals live at **both** levels, because different pacts genuinely have different red lines:

- **Org criticals** (step 1) are the firm's standing policy — defaults that flow into every pact of the type.
- **Pact criticals** belong to one deal: (a) **pact-only criticals**, written for this deal alone ("this property's anchor-tenant lease has at least 5 years remaining") with the same words→confirmed-structure flow; and (b) **overrides** of an org critical for this pact — tighten it, loosen it, or switch it off for this deal. An override is a first-class, stamped record (who, when, and a required reason) shown next to the org standard it replaces: "Org standard: cap rate ≥ 6% — this pact: ≥ 5.5% (override, reason recorded)". Governance stays honest: loosening a firm red line for a deal is possible, never invisible.
- **Evaluation on a pact = the org set as overridden, plus the pact-only set.** The panel groups rows by origin — *Org standard* vs *This pact* — and a Bad on an org-standard critical is flagged as a **policy breach** in the panel and the investment-committee step (T-118), weightier than a deal-specific miss.
- **Promotion:** a pact critical can be promoted to an org standard in one action ("make this an org critical"), carrying its confirmed structure into the library for future pacts of the type. Pact criticals are versioned within their pact and never leak into other pacts except by promotion.

## Demo and tests

- The Multifamily demo gains org criticals (cap rate floor; occupancy ≥90% across the last 3 years) evaluated against the existing demo data — including one critical the demo deal **fails** on its planted occupancy trend, rated Bad with the per-year figures shown.
- Tests: NL→structure confirmation gate (an unconfirmed critical evaluates nothing); a trend critical over 3 imported years rated from recomputed figures; a citation-backed text metric; the No-evidence state naming the missing document; wall test (seller side sees no criticals surface); bridge test (Bad → −1 finding; Good → ledger only).

## Acceptance

- An org admin writes "cap rate must be at least 6%" in words, confirms the structured form, and the multifamily demo pact rates it with the recomputed cap rate and its source figures one click away.
- The occupancy critical rates Bad on the demo's planted trend, raises a −1 finding, and appears in the bridge — while the seller's view of the same pact shows none of it.
- A critical with no supporting documents in the pact reads No evidence and names what is missing.
- On one pact, an org cap-rate critical is overridden from 6% to 5.5% with a reason and rates against 5.5% with the override shown; a second pact of the same type still rates against the org's 6%.
