## Why

DTC deals are priced on LTV claims and revenue that may run through accounts the seller personally owns. The evidence exists — storefront, marketplace and ad-platform exports — but no pack reads it.

## What this pack adds (the delta only)

1. **Channel revenue verification** (`packages/calculations`): revenue by channel recomputed from storefront/marketplace exports and reconciled to the P&L by year.
2. **Cohort value recompute:** CAC and LTV recomputed from order and ad-spend exports on a contribution-margin basis; the deck's LTV claim is a claims-register entry the recompute verifies or contradicts.
3. **Platform dependence:** share of revenue per platform, and — from documents — who owns the storefront, marketplace and ad accounts; a material account owned personally by the founder is a finding.
4. **Returns and inventory:** return-rate trend from exports; inventory ageing from stock reports, with aged stock priced as a finding.

- **Exports and documents only:** no live scraping of marketplaces or ad platforms; absence of an export is shown as absence.

## Multi-year P&L and trend (Kumar, 2026-11-26 — every pack)
The pack must take imported P&L statements for multiple fiscal years through the platform import system (statements by year) and use all of them: QoE and the valuation bridge read every imported year, and the pack produces trend findings — revenue growth, gross/operating margin drift, and the vertical's key expense ratios across years — each figure traceable to the import it came from. A single-year import still works (trend findings simply report one year). Withdrawn imports are excluded from trend reads like every other read path. The demo data includes at least three years of P&L with one planted trend defect that a single-year view would miss, and the acceptance test proves it surfaces.
