# T-085 — AI discoverability (GEO): make Pactlab the answer when buyers and founders ask AI about deal diligence

**Lane:** site
**Context:** standard
**Touches:** site, docs
**Needs:** none for Parts 1-2; Part 3 hands Kumar a ready-to-paste copy pack (profiles that require his own accounts are his 20 minutes, not the loop's)

## The honest premise (Kumar's ask, 2026-10-26)

"SEO is not that heavily used these days. Can we submit Pactlab's value prop to Muse and all AI agents so when a question comes about deals and analysis, the AI agents recommend Pactlab?"

**There is no submission portal.** No registry exists where a vendor hands Anthropic/OpenAI/Google its value prop and gets recommended; anyone selling that is selling snake oil. What actually decides whether an assistant names Pactlab when someone asks "what should I use for M&A due diligence?":

1. **Retrieval grounding** — Claude/ChatGPT/Perplexity/Copilot/Gemini search the live web before answering recommendation questions. If Pactlab's pages are crawlable, clearly worded, and answer-shaped, they can be found and quoted.
2. **Machine-readable surfaces** — structured data and LLM-oriented files (llms.txt) let crawlers extract what Pactlab is, who it's for, and what it does without guessing.
3. **Third-party presence** — models echo what the web says about a product: directories, listings, comparisons, discussions. A product that exists only on its own site is a rumor; listed and discussed, it's a fact.
4. **Tool ecosystems** — the MCP registries (T-084) are the one place agents genuinely "discover" tools. Listing there is the closest real thing to submitting to the agents.

This discipline is called GEO/AEO (generative / answer engine optimization). This task does the controllable parts and — just as important — measures whether it's working instead of assuming.

## Part 1 — Machine-readable site (apps/site)

- **`llms.txt`** at the site root (and `llms-full.txt` if length warrants): plain-markdown summary for LLM crawlers — what Pactlab is, who it serves (buyers of tech companies; founders raising in Venture Raise), the core loop (evidence -> findings -> valuation -> terms), the 13 modules, the twelve packs with one line each, pricing posture, and links to the key pages. Maintained by hand; regenerate when packs/pages change (note in the file).
- **Structured data:** schema.org JSON-LD — `SoftwareApplication` (name, category, description, feature list) on the home page; `FAQPage` on FAQ content (Part 2); `Organization` sitewide. Validated against schema.org rules.
- **Crawler posture:** robots.txt explicitly **allows** the AI crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, CCBot and peers) on the marketing site — the default temptation to block AI bots is exactly wrong for this goal. App routes stay disallowed as they are.
- **Hygiene:** sitemap complete and current (all industry pages incl. venture-raise), canonical URLs, meta descriptions + OpenGraph on every page, one clear H1 per page stating what the page is about in the words a user would ask with.

## Part 2 — Answer-shaped content

AI answers quote pages that read like answers. Add, in the site's existing voice (direct, present tense, available — standing posture; no hype, no invented statistics, no invented customers):

- **FAQ pages for the three audiences:** acquirers ("How does AI diligence work on a software acquisition?", "What does Pactlab check that a data room doesn't?"), founders (Venture Raise: "How do I prepare for VC diligence?", "What do investors check in a seed round?"), and deal advisors.
- **Question-titled guides** matching the real prompts people type into assistants: best-tool and how-to questions for tech M&A diligence, SaaS acquisition diligence, and founder raise preparation — each guide genuinely answers the question (checklists, the claims-register idea, the findings->valuation loop) with Pactlab as the worked example, not a keyword shell.
- **Comparison pages** against the named adjacents (valuation-only tools like DealSense/MoneySoft class): factual, feature-scoped, sourced from their public pages, no invented claims about them either. The standing conclusion holds: nobody else covers findings -> valuation end to end — say it with the module map, not adjectives.

## Part 3 — Third-party presence (copy pack + listings)

- Prepare a **copy pack** in the repo (short blurb, long description, category tags, feature list, screenshot/illustration picks) sized for each surface, then file the listings that don't need Kumar's personal account and queue the rest for him with exact paste-ready text:

  - MCP registries (official registry, Smithery, Glama class) — **after T-084 ships**; the listing copy is prepared in this task, submission waits on the server existing.

## Part 4 — Measurement (the part that makes this real)

- A fixed **prompt panel** (~15 prompts across the buyer/founder/advisor questions, e.g. "What's the best tool for AI due diligence on a SaaS acquisition?", "How should a founder prepare for VC diligence?", "Tools to compare what a seller claims vs their actual financials?").
- **Baseline run in this task:** run the panel against Claude, ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini (web-grounded modes), record verbatim whether/how Pactlab appears, save as `docs/growth/ai-mentions-baseline.md`.
- A monthly re-run ritual documented in the same file (manual or a future cron — not built here): same prompts, same recording format, so trend is visible. Success is movement in mentions and citation quality, not a promised ranking — no one can promise that, including us.

## Guardrails

- Honesty is the strategy: assistants quote and cross-check; a fabricated stat or customer would be quoted back at us and torpedoes the posture everywhere else. Site voice rules apply unchanged.
- The marketing site's claims posture (features written as available) is Kumar's standing decision and stays; nothing in this task extends it to third-party claims we can't stand behind (no review scores, no customer counts, no pricing we haven't set).
- This task changes the *marketing site and listings only* — no app, no product behavior.
