## Why

Fintech deals are priced on volume and growth claims that live in processor exports, and on licences that can lapse quietly. No current pack checks either: Software & SaaS reads code and billing, not regulated money movement.

## What this pack adds (the delta only)

1. **Volume and take-rate verification** (`packages/calculations`): processed volume, transaction counts and effective take rate recomputed from processor/bank exports and reconciled to the deck and the P&L.
2. **Deck claims register** (the T-083/T-141 pattern): TPV, growth, take rate and retention claims mapped to evidence, VERIFIED / CONTRADICTED / UNVERIFIED.
3. **Licence and sponsor register:** money-transmitter, lending and other state/federal licences plus sponsor-bank agreements, each with status and expiry from documents; a lapse or a near expiry is a finding priced into terms.
4. **Loss-rate findings:** chargeback and fraud/credit-loss rates by period from exports, against the thresholds the documents themselves state (network monitoring thresholds, sponsor covenants); a breach or a rising trend is a finding.
5. **Compliance evidence presence:** PCI attestation, KYC/AML program and exam/consent-order documents as evidence with status; absence shown as absence.

## Guardrails

- **No legal conclusions:** the pack reports licence status and dates from documents; it never opines on whether a licence is required or a program is compliant.
- **Evidence and absence:** figures come only from documents and adapters; absence is shown as absence, never filled in.
- **Marketing posture:** the site industry page is already written as available; verify it against the built pack and flag mismatches in the PR.

## Multi-year P&L and trend (Kumar, 2026-11-26 — every pack)
The pack must take imported P&L statements for multiple fiscal years through the platform import system (statements by year) and use all of them: QoE and the valuation bridge read every imported year, and the pack produces trend findings — revenue growth, gross/operating margin drift, and the vertical's key expense ratios across years — each figure traceable to the import it came from. A single-year import still works (trend findings simply report one year). Withdrawn imports are excluded from trend reads like every other read path. The demo data includes at least three years of P&L with one planted trend defect that a single-year view would miss, and the acceptance test proves it surfaces.
