# T-175 — Chat assistant on the public site and in the app: feature help, steps and confusion, in the user's language

Engine: claude
Model: opus
Lane: platform
Context: deep
Touches: apps/site apps/web apps/api packages/ai packages/contracts docs tasks
Needs: none
Checks: pnpm lint && pnpm typecheck && pnpm test && pnpm test:integration && pnpm test:fixtures && pnpm test:e2e

## Why

Kumar (2026-11-26): both the public site and the logged-in app get a chat option that explains features, walks users through steps, and clears up confusion. Built first-party on Pactlab's own AI stack — not a third-party support widget: no per-seat SaaS commit (pay-as-you-go rule), no outside vendor receiving conversations from a product whose brand is confidentiality, and the assistant answers from Pactlab's own corpus through the same model gateway, routing and ADR 0034 gates as every other AI job.

## Build

1. **One chat service, two surfaces.** A `support_chat` job in the JOB_MODEL_MAP (fast lane default — Haiku/Flash class — with the standard lane chains and per-message cost metering against the AI cost budget). The same backend serves a chat panel on the marketing site and in the web app; the UI components are shared, styled per surface.
2. **Grounding corpus — product knowledge only.** Retrieval over Pactlab's own published knowledge: site guides, FAQ, how-it-works, industry-page walkthroughs, the What's-new feature cards (T-155) and the in-app help/step content. Answers cite the page or guide they came from. **Hard boundary: the assistant has no access to any pact, deal, document or finding.** In-app it may use the caller's current page/route as context ("you're on the imports page — here's how sheet mapping works") and nothing else; it never reads or reveals org data, its own or anyone else's. If a question needs account- or deal-specific facts, it says so and offers the email route instead of guessing.
3. **Steps mode:** for "how do I…" questions it answers as numbered steps matched to the actual UI (from the help corpus), with a link that deep-links to the surface it describes.
4. **Honesty and fallback:** when the corpus has no answer, the assistant says it doesn't know and offers to pass the question to the team by email (creating the message draft with the conversation attached, sent only with the visitor's confirmation and, on the public site, their email address). It never invents a feature, a price, or a date. Marketing posture applies to its answers on the public site (present tense, available), sourced from the corpus — the corpus is the control.
5. **Identity and scope by surface:** public-site chat is anonymous (no login, no tracking beyond the conversation; rate-limited per session/IP). In-app chat knows the user's org role only to pitch steps at the right surface (buyer/seller/admin), never their content.
6. **Locale:** the assistant answers in the language of the page the visitor is on (site locale from T-171, app locale from T-172), from the localized corpus where it exists; where a locale's corpus is not yet live, it answers in English and says so. Language is a recorded job parameter, per T-174's pattern; ADR 0034 evaluation is per-language as there.
7. **Feature-request capture hooks live here, the pipeline is T-176:** when a visitor asks for something the product doesn't do, the assistant recognizes the feature request, confirms the wording back ("You'd like X — shall I log that for the team?"), and on confirmation hands it to T-176's intake with the conversation context. It never promises the feature will be built or when.

## Checks

- Grounding tests: a fixed question set answered from the corpus with citations; out-of-corpus questions produce the don't-know + email offer, never an invented answer.
- Boundary tests: prompts attempting to extract pact/org data (including the caller's own deal contents) are refused with the explanation path; the job's retrieval index provably contains only the knowledge corpus.
- Cost: per-message metering recorded like other AI jobs.

## Acceptance

- A visitor on pactlab.ai asks how valuation works and gets a cited answer from the guides; asks for a feature that doesn't exist and is offered the log-it path.
- A logged-in user on the imports page asks how to map a sheet and gets the correct steps with a deep link.
- Asking the in-app assistant about any deal's findings returns the boundary explanation, in the user's locale.
