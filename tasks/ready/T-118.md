# T-118 — Investment committee decision step for the buyer organization

Model: opus
Engine: claude
**Lane:** core
**Context:** deep
**Touches:** packages/domain (roles, pact lifecycle), packages/db, packages/contracts, apps/api (IC pack generation, decision record), apps/web (IC view, decision action, pact gating), docs (ADR)
**Needs:** T-110 (reports machinery — the IC pack reuses its generation/versioning), T-107 (two-sided agreement and close — the gate lands there)

## Why

External review (Kumar's PE contact, 2026-11-11): "Investment committee
decides the deal." Verified against the spec: the investment committee
exists only as a read-only *viewer* role ("reads approved conclusions,
scenarios and cited evidence"). In a PE/corp-dev workflow the IC is the
decision event — the deal lead does not commit the firm; the committee
does, on the record. Pactlab has no IC pack, no recorded decision, and no
gate between diligence/valuation and signing.

## What

1. **IC pack.** For a pact, the deal team generates an investment-committee
   pack from the pact's current state: the valuation scenario (with the
   persona-stamped run, T-105), findings posture and top risks, proposed
   terms, financing posture (T-117), and open blockers. Generation reuses
   the T-110 reports machinery: every generation is an immutable,
   versioned artifact stamped with what it captured, so what the
   committee saw is exactly what is on record.
2. **Decision record.** A user with the investment-committee role records
   the decision on a pack version: APPROVE, DECLINE, or
   APPROVE_WITH_CONDITIONS, with a note; conditions become pact blockers
   until the deal team marks them addressed and (if the org requires it)
   the IC clears them. Decisions are audit-logged and shown in the pact
   timeline and in the buyer org's detailed report (T-110).
3. **Gate.** A pact can be marked "IC approval required" (org default,
   overridable per pact). While required and unapproved, the pact cannot
   proceed to the two-sided agreement/signing step (T-107). A DECLINE
   moves the pact to a declined state; the deal team can revise and
   generate a fresh pack for reconsideration — each submission and
   decision stays on the record.
4. **Roles.** The existing investment-committee viewer role gains the
   decision action; viewers without the role still only read. IC members
   see packs for their own organization only.

## Guardrails

- The IC pack and decision are buyer-organization artifacts: nothing
  about them (existence, conditions, votes, notes) is ever visible to
  the seller or to bidders; the seller's flow is unchanged whether or
  not a buyer's IC exists.
- The pack is generated from recorded state (valuation run ids, findings
  counts, financing posture) — no hand-edited numbers; regenerating
  after a change produces a new version, never edits the old one.
- No voting mechanics beyond the recorded decision (no quorum rules, no
  per-member tallies) in v1: one authorized IC decision per submission,
  fully attributed.

## Acceptance

- Deal team can generate a versioned IC pack for a pact; an IC-role user
  can approve / decline / approve-with-conditions against a specific
  pack version, with the decision, decider, and timestamp on the pact
  timeline and audit log.
- With IC approval required and no approval recorded, attempting to
  start the T-107 agreement/signing step is refused with a clear reason;
  after approval it proceeds. Conditions appear as blockers.
- A declined pact can be revised and resubmitted with a new pack
  version; prior submissions and decisions remain visible to the buyer
  org.
- Tests prove the seller and other bidders see no IC artifacts on any
  surface, including reports (text-extraction confidentiality tests
  like T-110's).
- An ADR records the IC gate in the pact lifecycle (where it sits
  relative to terms agreement and the T-107 close).
