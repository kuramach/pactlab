# T-097 — Seller-led sale: the seller opens the pact and runs a bid process

**Lane:** core
**Context:** deep
**Touches:** domain, db, contracts, api, web, e2e, docs/adr
**Needs:** none

## Why (Kumar, 2026-10-28)

Today every pact starts on the buy side: an acquirer opens it and diligences
a target. Deals also run the other way — a seller (or its banker) opens the
process and invites several buyers: PE firms, corp-dev teams, strategic
acquirers. This is the shape of a VC raise too (ADR 0012), where the founder
runs the round and several investors bid. Each invited buyer looks at what the
seller shares, asks questions, then **submits an offer or passes**. Both
directions must work; the buyer-led flow stays as it is.

## Model (build on what exists, don't fork it)

- **Who opens the pact is a pact property:** `initiatedBy: BUYER | SELLER`,
  chosen on New pact. Buyer-led = today's flow, unchanged.
- **Seller-led pact:** the seller's org owns the pact. Its team holds the owner
  roles (as the founder does in ADR 0012). Buyers join as **bidders** through
  the ADR 0014 invitation path (guest deal memberships, sign in their own way).
  They never join the seller's org.
- **Bidders are isolated from each other.** This is the new confidentiality
  boundary and the core of the task. Bidder A never sees Bidder B: not their
  identity, questions, answers given privately to them, offer, or pass. Today
  RLS has two sides (`app_is_buyer_side`). A seller-led pact needs a
  per-bidder scope. Write it as an ADR (0015) and prove it with RLS
  integration tests before building the UI.
- **Generalize Venture Raise offers.** `venture_offers` already models
  competing bids inside one pact, compared side by side with no ranking. Lift
  that into a general offer model used by both seller-led M&A and Venture
  Raise. Don't build a second offer system. Venture Raise moves onto it with
  its behaviour unchanged.

## The bidder's journey

1. **Invite.** The seller invites bidders by email (one bidder = one firm; it
   can have several people). Optional NDA acknowledgement before anything
   else is visible.
2. **Look.** The bidder sees the seller's **shared data room** (documents and
   numbers the seller marked as shared). That's the allow-list, like the ADR
   0012 investor view. Seller-confidential analysis never reaches a bidder.
3. **Ask.** Q&A: the bidder asks questions. The seller answers each one
   **privately** (to that bidder only) or **to all bidders**. An answer to all
   is anonymised: the question shows without naming who asked.
4. **Decide.** The bidder **submits an offer** (price, structure, conditions,
   validity date; optional attachment) or **passes** (with an optional
   reason). It can revise its offer until the seller's deadline. Every
   version is kept.
5. **Hear back.** The bidder sees the status of its own bid only (received /
   under review / shortlisted / declined / won).

## The seller's side

- **Bidders board:** each bidder's stage (invited → NDA → active → offered /
  passed), last activity, open questions.
- **Process settings:** offer deadline, rounds (first-round indicative →
  second-round binding is the common pattern; support at least two rounds),
  and who's shortlisted into the next round.
- **Offers compared side by side** using the existing comparison, with mechanics
  shown and no ranking or recommendation (ADR 0012 decision 6 holds:
  explain, never advise).
- **Q&A inbox** across bidders, with answer-private / answer-to-all.
- **Award:** marking a winner moves the pact into the normal diligence flow
  with that buyer. Other bidders are declined, and their access ends (their
  own offers stay in their records).

## Guardrails

- No bidder ever sees another bidder's anything. Every new table gets RLS +
  integration tests for the bidder-to-bidder case and the bidder-to-seller-
  confidential case.
- Buyer-led pacts behave exactly as before. Existing e2e stays green.
- Venture Raise keeps its current behaviour after moving to the shared offer
  model.

## Decisions for Kumar (stop and flag in the PR if the build forces one)

- Can a bidder bring its own team (several users per bidding firm)? Default: yes.
- Is NDA acknowledgement a click-through, or an uploaded signed NDA? Default:
  click-through with the NDA document attached.
- Once a winner is chosen, does the winner's own buy-side diligence happen in
  the same pact (shared with the seller under the normal two-side rules) or in
  a new buyer-owned pact linked to it? Default: same pact.

## Retest checklist

1. Seller creates a seller-led pact, shares 5 documents, invites 3 bidders.
2. Bidder A asks a question; seller answers privately. Bidder B can't see it.
   Seller answers another to all. Both see it, without the asker's name.
3. Bidder A submits an offer, revises it. Bidder B passes with a reason.
   Bidder C offers.
4. Seller sees all three on the board and A + C side by side. Neither bidder
   can see the other's offer or whether the other passed.
5. Seller shortlists A into round 2, then awards. C is declined and loses
   access. A continues into diligence.
6. A buyer-led pact still works end to end as before. A Venture Raise round
   still compares offers as before.
