## Why

Small clinics trade on the same evidence as larger provider groups but the risks concentrate: one owner-operator, one referral source, one lease. A scaled-down pack that asks exactly those questions.

## What this pack adds (the delta only)

1. **The T-160 evidence set, scaled:** payer mix, denials trend, credentialing register for the (small) roster — reused, not rebuilt.
2. **Owner-operator dependence:** share of visits and collections delivered by the selling owner, from schedule/billing exports; no succession or associate coverage is a finding.
3. **Referral concentration:** visits by referral source recomputed from exports; a single dominant source is a finding.
4. **Premises:** the clinic lease as evidence — remaining term, renewal options, assignment on sale; a short remaining term without renewal is a finding priced as relocation/renegotiation risk.

## Multi-year P&L and trend (Kumar, 2026-11-26 — every pack)
The pack must take imported P&L statements for multiple fiscal years through the platform import system (statements by year) and use all of them: QoE and the valuation bridge read every imported year, and the pack produces trend findings — revenue growth, gross/operating margin drift, and the vertical's key expense ratios across years — each figure traceable to the import it came from. A single-year import still works (trend findings simply report one year). Withdrawn imports are excluded from trend reads like every other read path. The demo data includes at least three years of P&L with one planted trend defect that a single-year view would miss, and the acceptance test proves it surfaces.
