# T-071 — The three phases of a pact: Pre-Diligence, Diligence, Post-Close

**Lane:** core
**Context:** deep
**Touches:** domain, db, api, web, spec, seed
**Needs:** none

## Kumar's model (2026-10-23, his words)

A pact has 3 stages. **Pre** — "to see if the deal even makes sense, are we too far off, not worth even getting into diligence." **In-flight** (he proposes calling it **Diligence**) — "where we cross the T's and line the ducks on what is being said and what is being observed in contracts, finances and more." **Post** — "we have completed the deal and now we need to make sure the assets we have identified are preserved and enhanced for success."

## Naming (decided in this task; labels are display strings and cheap to change)

- **Pre-Diligence** (phase key `PRE`) — the go/no-go screen.
- **Diligence** (phase key `DILIGENCE`) — the in-flight phase. Kumar's instinct to just call it Diligence is right; "in-flight" adds a word without adding meaning.
- **Post-Close** (phase key `POST`) — the deal is done; the work is preservation and enhancement.

## What exists (do not rebuild)

- Deals already carry a pipeline `stage` enum in the domain/API layer: `SCREENING, DILIGENCE, NEGOTIATION, SIGNED, CLOSED`, plus a separate `status` (`ACTIVE, ON_HOLD, ARCHIVED`). Transaction type is fixed at creation. Audit log exists. The evidence -> finding -> valuation -> terms core loop exists and is the Diligence phase's engine.
- What does NOT exist: any phase behavior. Today the stages are labels a user can patch; nothing about the product changes when a deal moves, CLOSED is a dead end, and screening has no verdict.

## What to build

1. **Phase mapping in the domain layer.** Add `DEAL_PHASES` next to `TRANSACTION_TYPES` in `@pactlab/domain`: `SCREENING -> PRE`; `DILIGENCE, NEGOTIATION, SIGNED -> DILIGENCE`; `CLOSED -> POST`. Phase is DERIVED from stage, never a second field to keep in sync. Deal header and deal lists show the phase (display names above) alongside the pipeline stage; lists can filter by phase.
2. **Pre-Diligence: the screen.** A screening panel on the deal, deliberately light: intake for teaser/CIM and headline financials, the asking price, a rough value-sanity view (asking vs a coarse range — explicitly NOT the price bridge), and a red-flag checklist. The phase ends in a recorded **screening verdict**: `GO` (promotes the deal to DILIGENCE, carrying screening notes and red flags forward as diligence lines of inquiry), `NO_GO` (archives the pact with the rationale), or `NEEDS_MORE` (stays in Pre with the gaps listed). Every verdict is audit-logged with its rationale. Guardrail: screening output is directional only — it never creates findings, never touches valuation, and is labelled as screening-grade wherever shown.
3. **Diligence: said vs observed.** Keep the existing core loop as-is. Add a **claims register**: seller assertions (from the CIM, management calls, the data room index — entered or imported as short statements) each tracked as `VERIFIED`, `CONTRADICTED`, or `UNVERIFIED` against evidence, linkable to the findings they produced. This is Kumar's "what is being said vs what is being observed," made a first-class view of the phase. The phase's exit is the existing flow: signed terms -> SIGNED -> CLOSED.
4. **Post-Close: preserve and enhance.** Entering CLOSED generates two registers FROM diligence output — no re-entry of data:
   - **Asset preservation register**: the assets diligence identified as carrying the value (key contracts, customers, people, IP, licences — sourced from evidence/findings tagged during diligence) each with a preservation action, owner, and review cadence.
   - **Post-close actions**: open findings and remediation, escrow, and indemnity items convert to tracked actions with owners and dates, including watch items for escrow releases and indemnity claim windows.
   Both registers are ordinary lists the deal team works; completing or lapsing items is audit-logged. Post is a working phase, not a tombstone.
5. **Continuity rule (the point of the model).** Nothing is re-typed between phases: screening red flags become diligence lines of inquiry; claims link to findings; findings and identified assets become post-close registers. Every conversion is a reference, not a copy.
6. **Spec.** Amend `docs/spec/Claude Code Specification.md` with the three-phase model, the stage->phase mapping, the screening verdict, the claims register, and the post-close registers. Seed fixtures: at least one pact per phase (a SCREENING pact with a verdict pending, a DILIGENCE pact with claims, a CLOSED pact with generated registers); update the seed fixtures test counts to match.

## Guardrails

- Do NOT change the stage enum or remove the raw stage patch — phase is a derived view plus phase behavior layered on it.
- Build only the phase surface above; reuse evidence, findings, valuation, and audit as they are. No new subsystem, no separate per-phase data silos.
- Screening stays light on purpose: if a screening feature needs deep evidence processing, it belongs in Diligence, not Pre.

## Acceptance

- Contract test pins the stage->phase mapping for all five stages; phase renders in the deal header and list filter.
- Screening verdict flow works end to end: GO promotes and carries red flags into diligence as lines of inquiry; NO_GO archives with rationale; verdicts appear in the audit log.
- Claims register: an assertion can be created, linked to evidence/findings, and moved between VERIFIED / CONTRADICTED / UNVERIFIED with the change audit-logged.
- Closing a deal with tagged assets and open findings generates both post-close registers by reference; no duplicate data entry.
- Spec amended; seed fixtures cover all three phases and fixture count tests updated.
