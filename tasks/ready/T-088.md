# T-088 — Marketing system: reach PE firms, corp dev teams, and founders — and run it continuously
Engine: codex

**Lane:** growth
**Context:** deep
**Touches:** site, docs
**Needs:** T-085 (being found: SEO/GEO — this task is the other half, going to them), T-087 (pricing — every motion below lands on a way to pay), T-083 (the founder product this markets)

## Kumar's ask (2026-10-26)

"A marketing task — how to market it and reach PE companies, corp dev teams and also founders, and how to run this continuously."

## Design constraint: a solo founder's bandwidth

Kumar is the company. This system is built to run on **~2-3 hours of his time per week** plus assets the build produces once and reuses. Anything needing daily attention is out. Founder-led, asset-heavy, cadence-light. Where a motion needs his face (outreach, events, calls), the task prepares everything up to the send/button — lists, drafts, pages — and he fires it.

## The three segments, and what each one buys

### 1. PE firms (lower/mid-market, tech-focused) — the repeat buyers

- **Their pain:** diligence is a recurring cost across many deals a year; quality varies by who ran it; deals die late after real money was spent. Pactlab's pitch: a Pre-diligence screen that kills bad deals before the spend (T-071), the same findings->valuation rigor on every deal, and a portfolio view across pacts. Product hooks: firm subscription (T-087), many-repos/scale (T-055/T-056).
- **Reach:** founder-led LinkedIn outreach to deal principals and associates (drafts + a target-account criteria list in the ops doc: tech-focused, lower/mid-market, active platform + add-on pace); the ACG (Association for Corporate Growth) community and events — the mid-market deal world's front door, incl. the Dallas chapter; referral partnerships with QoE/FDD providers and M&A advisors who sit in these deals already and don't compete with the findings->valuation loop.
- **Asset:** PE landing page (below) + the deal-screening lead magnet.

### 2. Corp dev teams (in-house M&A at serial acquirers) — the workflow buyers

- **Their pain:** diligence knowledge scatters across data rooms, email, and whoever ran the last deal; post-close, nobody tracks whether the assets the deal was bought for survived. Pitch: one register from screen to post-close, and the Post phase (preserve-and-enhance) as the differentiator — corp dev owns integration outcomes, so Post is their feature.
- **Reach:** LinkedIn content + outreach to heads of corp dev / M&A; corp-dev communities and roundtables (virtual first); a quarterly webinar built from product material ("What your diligence found vs what integration kept") — run as a recurring series, recorded once, reused as content.
- **Asset:** corp dev landing page + the post-close lead magnet.

### 3. Founders raising (Venture Raise) — the volume segment, opposite direction

- **Their pain:** they get diligenced blind — they find out what investors think of their cap table and metrics during the raise, when it's expensive. Pitch (T-083's own words): see what the investor will find, first.
- **Reach:** founder-dense surfaces — accelerator/incubator partnerships (the channel *is* the customer: accelerators hand Venture Raise to their cohorts; one partnership = a cohort of founders), founder communities and newsletters, Product Hunt at the right moment (queued in T-085), founder-education content ("What VCs actually check in your data room") that teaches the category.
- **Asset:** founder landing page (the venture-raise industry page carries most of this; the landing page is the campaign front door) + the raise-readiness checklist.

## Buildable deliverables (what the loop produces)

1. **Three segment landing pages** on apps/site (`/for/pe-firms`, `/for/corp-dev`, `/for/founders`): segment pain, the specific product truths that answer it, the demo CTA, pricing-path link (T-087). Site voice and posture rules apply; in sitemap + llms.txt (coordinate with T-085).
2. **Three lead magnets**, generated from real product content (packs + T-071 + T-083): the *Pre-Diligence Screening Checklist* (PE/buyer), the *Post-Close Asset Preservation Guide* (corp dev), the *Raise Readiness Checklist* (founders). Downloadable PDFs behind an email form — the form feeds the nurture sequences, not a void.
3. **Demo entry:** every page's CTA lands on one action — run the HealthyCo **demo pact** (synthetic data, self-serve where the showcase env allows; otherwise a booked walkthrough). One funnel, one verb everywhere: *run a pact*.
4. **Nurture sequences:** 4-5 emails per segment (copy in repo), each teaching one real thing (claims register, pool shuffle, screening verdict) and ending at the demo CTA. Sending runs on standard email tooling Kumar picks at build; the copy and the trigger map ship here.
5. **Content calendar (first 8 weeks, then a rolling template):** one substantive piece per week derived from product material — pack spotlights, a module explained, a checklist expanded — each repurposed as 2-3 LinkedIn posts (drafts in the calendar, Kumar posts). T-085's prompt panel feeds topics: the questions buyers ask AI assistants *are* the content backlog.
6. **The marketing ops doc** (`docs/growth/ops.md`): the cadence below, the target-account criteria per segment, the partnership target types, the event list, and the metrics ritual. This doc is the "continuously" — the task isn't done until the loop that runs after it is written down.

## The continuous cadence (what "run it" actually means)

- **Weekly (~1 hr of Kumar):** one content piece ships; one outreach batch (10-15 accounts, one segment, rotating); pipeline glance.
- **Monthly (~1 hr):** metrics review — site traffic (T-085 analytics), demo pacts started, calls booked, nurture conversions, AI-mention panel re-run (T-085 Part 5); kill the weakest channel, double the strongest.
- **Quarterly (~half day):** one live moment — webinar, ACG event, or accelerator cohort session; pricing/packaging feedback from real conversations fed back to T-087's price book; calendar for next quarter set from what the metrics said.
- Every lead, partner, and account lives in one pipeline table (tool of Kumar's choice at build; the ops doc defines the fields and stages) — if it isn't in the table, it didn't happen.

## Guardrails

- **No invented proof.** No fake case studies, no borrowed logos, no made-up deal counts or stats — in copy, magnets, or outreach drafts. The product's honesty is the brand; marketing that lies would be quoted back by the same AI systems T-085 courts.
- No spam mechanics: outreach is small-batch, named-account, founder-signed. No purchased lists, no mass automation, no planted community posts.
- Marketing copy follows the standing site posture (features as available) but never extends it into claims about customers, results, or traction.
- Spend starts at zero: every channel above is time-and-assets until the monthly review proves one worth paying for; paid anything requires Kumar's explicit decision with the metric that justifies it.

## Acceptance

- Three segment landing pages live in the site pattern, each with the demo CTA and pricing path; three lead-magnet PDFs generated from product content, gated by a working capture form.
- Nurture sequences (3 segments x 4-5 emails) committed as copy + trigger map.
- 8-week content calendar committed with drafts or full briefs per piece; ops doc committed with cadence, target-account criteria, partnership/event lists, pipeline fields, and the metrics ritual.
- A dry-run of the weekly cadence is possible from the ops doc alone: a reader can tell what ships this week, who it goes to, and what gets measured on Friday.
