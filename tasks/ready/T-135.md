# T-135 — QoE work product: adjusted-EBITDA normalization, add-back verification, working-capital peg
Engine: claude

Lane: core (financials / packs)
Context: deep
Touches: packages/domain packages/db apps/api apps/web docs

**Needs note:** nothing. Reuses the T-094 Finances uploads, the findings/direction machinery, and the T-110 report machinery — do not build parallel versions of any of them.

## Origin

Feedback from Kumar's PE friend (2026-11-11/12), checked against the spec at the time: Pactlab's QoE coverage is partial. What a QoE firm actually signs — an adjusted-EBITDA normalization schedule, verification of claimed add-backs, and a working-capital analysis — has no first-class work product in the platform. (Practitioner anchor recorded in docs/pricing-research.md: a standalone QoE runs $20k–$50k.) Kumar approved filing this on 2026-11-17.

## Build

1. **Adjusted-EBITDA normalization schedule.** Reported EBITDA -> normalization adjustments -> adjusted EBITDA, presented as a versioned schedule. Adjustment categories at minimum: owner/executive compensation normalization, one-time and non-recurring items, related-party transactions, rent normalization, pro-forma adjustments (acquisitions, new locations/contracts). Every adjustment is an evidence-backed finding: source document + citation, amount, direction — and it enters the valuation bridge only through the existing VALUATION_DIRECTION_MAP rules. Persona rules are unchanged (personas translate findings; they never alter the schedule).
2. **Add-back register.** Every seller-claimed add-back is a register row: claimed amount, seller's stated rationale, supporting evidence, verification status (VERIFIED / PARTIALLY_VERIFIED / UNVERIFIED), and recurring-vs-one-off classification. Unverified add-backs are flagged in the schedule and excluded from adjusted EBITDA by default; including one is an explicit reviewer decision, recorded. Nothing is silently accepted.
3. **Working-capital analysis.** Normalized working capital from the uploaded balance sheets across the trailing periods available: average and seasonal range, the components driving movement, and a proposed peg (target working capital) with its derivation shown. The peg feeds a term proposal through the existing term machinery (a working-capital peg / true-up mechanic) — it never edits the bridge directly.
4. **QoE report section.** The schedule, register, and working-capital analysis render as a section of the pact report via the T-110 machinery (versioned, branded, confidentiality enforced in the read model as elsewhere). Buyer-side confidentiality applies per the existing model.
5. **Deterministic numbers.** All arithmetic runs through the existing financial calculation layer (decimal, versioned) — models locate and cite evidence; they never compute the schedule. Every figure in the schedule traces to a cited source value.

## Guardrails

- The work product is labelled as platform-prepared analysis. Pactlab is not a QoE firm and does not issue an independent QoE opinion; the schedule is built for the buyer's own review and for their advisors.
- Said vs Observed: seller-claimed figures and verified figures are never merged into one number — the register's status column is the distinction, carried into the report.

## Acceptance

- A pact with uploaded financials produces the three-part work product (schedule, register, working-capital analysis with peg) with every adjustment and add-back cited.
- An unverified add-back demonstrably stays out of adjusted EBITDA until a reviewer explicitly includes it, and the inclusion is recorded.
- The QoE section appears in the generated pact report under the existing confidentiality rules, with PDF text-extraction test coverage like T-110's.
- Contract tests: unmapped adjustment categories fail the build (registry pattern), and the bridge receives QoE inputs only via the direction map.
